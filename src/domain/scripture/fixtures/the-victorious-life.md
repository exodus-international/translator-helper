# A Reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Romans
 
*Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword. As it is written, "For thy sake we are being killed all the day long; we are regarded as sheep to be slaughtered." No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us. For I am sure that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers, nor height, nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.*

# Reflection

On our last day, it will be good to revisit a Scripture verse, a word from the Lord that has been a recurring theme throughout the month.
The Letter to the Hebrews states: “Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself [Jesus] likewise partook of the same nature, that through death he might destroy him who has the power of death, that is, the devil, and deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong bondage” (Hebrews 2:14–15).
The passage tells us a number of significant things: first, that the devil holds the power of death over the human race; second, that fear of death has been an enslaving state for the whole of humanity; and third, that Jesus has destroyed the devil’s deadly power.
This is a wonderful deliverance, and the source of our freedom as Jesus’s disciples.
Death, that fearsome enemy, has lost its mortal sting, and we no longer need to fear it.
As we go forward, let’s continue to grow into that freedom that our good Master has gained for us.

St. Cyprian of Carthage, a 3rd-century bishop and martyr who lived during a fierce Roman persecution, saw martyrdom coming toward him and wrote stirringly to his people to encourage them to stand strong as they faced the possibility of suffering for their faith: “Let all our people fix their minds not on death but rather on immortality; let them commit themselves to the Lord in complete faith and unflinching courage and make their confession with joy rather than in fear, knowing that in this contest the soldiers of God and Christ are not slain, but rather win their crowns.”

Taking our cue from Cyprian, let us live as free, courageous men who walk in the victory of Jesus.
Let us refuse to be overcome by our own weaknesses and sins.
Let us put to flight the lies of the devil.
Let us battle against our lingering fear of suffering and death.
And let us again stir up our love and loyalty for the one who has granted us this great freedom.
We need not pretend that the prospect of death is easy, but we can stand in the victory won by Christ.
With supreme confidence in the love and power of God, let us make our way through the veil of death to the life that beckons beyond.
And if, like Jesus at the grave of Lazarus, we occasionally find ourselves troubled and in tears at death’s dark face, let us gather our courage and, with firm hope, proclaim the Christian victory chant with all the saints of heaven: “O Death! Where is your victory?”

*Let us pray.*
